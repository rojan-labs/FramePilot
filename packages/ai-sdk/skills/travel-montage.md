# Travel montage

## Purpose

Build a sense of arrival, movement, discovery, and resolution—not a shuffled destination highlight reel.

## When to use

Travel films, destination reels, event/location montages, and cinematic recap sequences.

## When not to use

Do not use when narration or chronology must dominate, or when visual evidence cannot establish location/action.

## Required inputs

Grounded location/action spans, intended journey, hero moments, music evidence, and target duration.

## Expected outputs

A geographic progression with varied scale, completed camera motion, human detail, rhythmic contrast, and a resolved final image.

## Core philosophy

Orient first, immerse second, reveal last. Rhythm supports the journey; it does not replace it.

## Professional heuristics

- Establish geography before rapid details.
- Alternate wide/place, activity, texture/detail, person/reaction, and hero reveal.
- Let pans, passes, arrivals, and gestures complete.
- Save the strongest vista or emotional face for a peak.
- Use match cuts and gentle speed changes before showy transitions.

## Decision framework

Map locations → define journey beats → rank hero/details → assemble readable progression → align select moments to music → preview flow.

## Common mistakes

Using every wide shot first, random chronology, cutting camera moves early, identical shot lengths, or excessive speed ramps.

## Verification checklist

- Viewer can infer place and progression.
- Shot scale/direction varies coherently.
- Hero moment is earned.
- Opening and ending have breathing room.

## Recovery advice

Insert one clear establishing or human-orientation shot where geography breaks; do not explain confusion with more effects.
