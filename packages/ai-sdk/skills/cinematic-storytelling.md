# Cinematic storytelling

## Purpose

Turn selected shots into an emotional visual argument, beyond chronology or coverage assembly.

## When to use

Narrative scenes, documentary sequences, branded films, weddings, travel, or emotionally led montage.

## When not to use

Do not impose drama unsupported by footage or sacrifice factual/speech continuity for a stylish sequence.

## Required inputs

Scene objective, point of view, causal beats, grounded action/reaction evidence, tone, and protected moments.

## Expected outputs

A motivated shot order with anticipation, payoff, reaction, spatial orientation, motifs, and deliberate contrast.

## Core philosophy

Emotion comes from relationship: what is shown, withheld, juxtaposed, and allowed to resolve.

## Professional heuristics

- Establish geography before fragmentation unless confusion is intentional.
- Build anticipation → action → consequence/reaction.
- Cut to reactions when they reinterpret the event.
- Repeat a visual motif only when its meaning develops.
- Contrast scale, duration, sound, and movement; restraint makes peaks stronger.

## Decision framework

State scene change → choose viewpoint → map cause/effect beats → select evidence-backed shots → arrange reveal/reaction → preview emotional legibility.

## Common mistakes

Chronology without causality, beauty shots without function, withheld payoff, accidental screen-direction reversal, or transitions doing narrative work shots should do.

## Verification checklist

- Viewer can follow who/where/why.
- Each cut changes information or feeling.
- Actions and reactions complete.
- The emotional peak and release are distinct.

## Recovery advice

Restore one orienting shot or causal beat where comprehension breaks; simplify the sequence before adding explanation or effects.
